# Font Thumbnail Design

Use the white-background font-name specimen and its matching complete prompt
from `visual-direction/theme.txt`. There is no old marketplace thumbnail in
this workflow. The specimen supplies the lettering; the prompt supplies the
font-specific visual direction.

## Preserve the font

- Preserve exact spelling, supplied capitalization, distinctive glyph shapes,
  spacing, and filled-versus-outline construction.
- Remove the specimen's white field; do not retype the name in a substitute
  font or force it into all caps.
- Keep the full name crisp, high-contrast, and readable at about 300 px wide.
- Create the 3:2, main 1:1, and storefront 1:1 images as separate compositions.

## Keep the design restrained

Follow the prompt's one visual premise. Use only details that explain it; the
composition may use negative space, but it should not default to a sterile
field. A quiet, relevant paper/ink/print texture or faint illustration can
make flat work feel tactile; keep it purposeful and secondary. Keep the
lettering flat and front-facing, separate from every photographed or rendered
surface. Compare the concept with recent thumbnails across medium, layout,
focal motif, palette, texture/light and title placement; avoid repeating a
generic premium still life or warm, hazy photography with a large staged object
or face on one side and empty title space on the other. Use that layout only
when the subject is specific to the font and feels balanced. Treat liked user
examples as quality signals, not templates. Avoid the rejected oversized initial
with orbit rings, split blue/berry descriptor panel, and arch/portal title frame. Vary the visual
idea and medium across a batch, not just the colors. Keep any face soft, cropped
or subdued behind the type. Avoid glitter, sparkles, starbursts, confetti,
particles, fake flares, arbitrary badges, gratuitous glow, excessive texture,
and generic AI decoration. The 33-reference audit distills the captions into
composition lessons, not prop lists.

## Generate and review

When finished artwork or a prototype is requested, use the built-in
image-generation tool with only the matching specimen and prompt. Generate each
ratio directly; do not resize, pad, or crop the 3:2 image into a square. Review
exact spelling, glyph fidelity, clipping, contrast, prompt fidelity, and
small-size legibility. Regenerate only when a clear defect needs correction.

Save each image separately at the path supplied by the user or workflow. Report
the path and any remaining visible issue.
