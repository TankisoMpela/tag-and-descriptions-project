# Font Thumbnail Design

Use the white-background font-name specimen and its matching complete prompt
from `visual-direction/theme.txt`. There is no old marketplace thumbnail in
this workflow. The specimen supplies the lettering; the prompt supplies the
font-specific visual direction.

## Preserve the font

- Preserve exact spelling, supplied capitalization, distinctive glyph shapes,
  spacing, and filled-versus-outline construction.
- Remove the specimen's white field; do not retype the name in a substitute
  font or force it into all caps.
- Keep the full name crisp, high-contrast, and readable at about 300 px wide.
- Create the 3:2, main 1:1, and storefront 1:1 images as separate compositions.

## Match the composition and keep it restrained

Follow the per-font composition decision and the 33-reference audit. Keep the
name visually first as a flat, front-facing overlay, separate from every
photographed or rendered surface. Compare the concept with recent thumbnails
across medium, layout, focal motif, palette, texture/light and title placement;
avoid repeating a generic premium still life or warm, hazy photography with a
large staged object or face on one side and empty title space on the other. Use
that layout only when the subject is specific to the font and the composition
feels balanced. Treat liked user examples as quality signals, not templates.
Avoid the rejected oversized initial with orbit rings, split blue/berry
descriptor panel, or arch/portal title frame.
Vary the visual idea and medium across a batch, not just the colors. If a person
appears, let the person recede: crop away the face or keep it blurred and low
contrast. Preserve only scene details that explain the premise. A flat design
may use a quiet, relevant paper/ink/print texture or faint illustration so it
does not feel sterile; keep it purposeful and secondary. No
glitter, sparkles, starbursts, confetti, particles, fake flares, arbitrary
badges, gratuitous glow, excessive texture, or generic AI decoration.

## Generate and review

When finished artwork or a prototype is requested, use the built-in
image-generation tool with only the matching specimen and prompt. Generate each
ratio directly; do not resize, pad, or crop the 3:2 image into a square. Review
exact spelling, glyph fidelity, clipping, contrast, prompt fidelity, and
small-size legibility. Regenerate only when a clear defect needs correction.

Save each image separately at the path supplied by the user or workflow. Report
the path and any remaining visible issue.
