# Next Workflow Pass — Main 3:2 Thumbnails

This pass covers the two raw white-background specimens that lacked finished thumbnails in the current prototype gallery. It has three individual render options: two distinct Evercourt treatments and one revised Vantrelle treatment. All use fresh specimens made from the actual font files, with natural title case.

## Workflow choices

- Start from the actual specimen, preserve its visible letterforms and natural capitalization, and keep the exact font name as the first read.
- Give the 3:2 thumbnail a concise, readable style descriptor beneath the name.
- Choose a font-specific image idea. A background can be photographic, illustrated, or graphic; it earns its place through meaning, balance, and contrast rather than a default “premium” treatment.
- Treat the seller's image examples as signals, not templates. Favor clear contrast, title focus, and a subject that fits the font; don't reuse a favorite palette or layout mechanically.
- Keep all lettering flat in the image plane, separate from objects and scenery. Avoid the recurring warm, hazy side-object/open-title composition when the font has no reason for it.
- Keep each selected option in the gallery easy to compare, with no collage.

## Evercourt

**Visible text:** Evercourt / A Flowing Calligraphy Script

The gallery includes two liked options: a quiet outdoor tennis-court photograph and a restrained screen-printed tennis-court illustration. Both make a coherent link to the name while keeping the connected script prominent. These are two distinct approaches, not a direction to repeat for unrelated fonts.

### Screen-printed illustration prompt

> Design one finished 3:2 landscape font specimen thumbnail. Use the attached white-background image as the source for the exact display lettering. The only visible copy must be exactly “Evercourt” and, below it, “A Flowing Calligraphy Script”. Spell the name Evercourt in natural title case; do not use all caps. Preserve the attached specimen's flowing connected calligraphy and looping capital E. Make the title large, fully visible, and readable at a small thumbnail size.
>
> The visual medium is a restrained vintage screen-printed illustration, unmistakably drawn and printed, not a photograph: slightly imperfect ink contours, broad hand-painted color shapes, simplified tree silhouettes and a simplified neighborhood tennis court. Use a quiet three-color palette of faded forest green, dusty blue-grey, and warm ivory paper. The court occupies the lower third with just a few thin cream court lines; a dark row of trees sits behind it; pale open sky fills the top. Add a single small mustard tennis ball near a corner of the court. Keep the scene spacious and calm, with a subtle paper tooth and a few natural ink registration imperfections only. No fine hatch overload, no scattered grunge flecks, no gradients, no photorealism, no lens effects, no 3D, no glossy or cinematic treatment.
>
> Lay the large warm-ivory word “Evercourt” flat across the dark tree band, centered and dominant; set the exact subtitle just beneath in a clean, smaller, dark-green sans serif against a narrow warm-ivory paper strip or pale sky area so it stays clearly legible. Both lines are flat 2D typography in the same illustration plane and must not bend with the court or appear painted onto its surface. Leave clear margins, no clipping, no extra objects or copy. Avoid badges, arches, circles, decorative frames, ornamental swashes beyond the font's own letterforms, sparkles, glitter, confetti, repeated graphic flourishes, and any AI-looking decorative excess.

## Vantrelle

**Visible text:** Vantrelle / A High Contrast Display Serif

**Concept:** A small letterpress proofing machine rendered as a limited-color printed illustration makes the setting about type-making rather than generic luxury or architecture. The press runs across the lower edge; the high-contrast serif occupies the open center.

**Final generation prompt:**

> Create one finished 3:2 landscape font thumbnail, using the attached white specimen as the exact source for the name lettering. Show exactly two lines of text: “Vantrelle” and, beneath it, “A High Contrast Display Serif”. Use natural title case. Preserve the specimen's elegant high-contrast display serif: slender hairlines, firm vertical strokes, sharp tapered serifs, distinctive e forms, and exact spacing/proportions. Do not thicken it into a bold face or replace it with a generic serif. Keep the complete title and subtitle clearly readable, with the font name as the strongest element.
>
> Build a specific visual idea around the craft of making type: a flat, hand-printed editorial illustration of a small working letterpress proof press, not a photograph. The press sits low in the frame, drawn as a simplified but recognizable horizontal machine with one roller, a paper bed, and a small crank; its parts span across the lower edge so there is no oversized object pushed to one side. The printshop image stays secondary and free of readable words or markings. Keep the upper two thirds a warm pale butter-ivory paper field, with quiet paper tooth and one restrained imperfect ink edge. Use deep indigo for the title against the light paper, with a clear vermilion accent only in the small press illustration or subtitle. The palette should feel bold and lively, not metallic or luxurious.
>
> Set “Vantrelle” large on one line across the central width of the open paper area, with balanced margins and a little breathing room around the hairlines. Put “A High Contrast Display Serif” directly below in a smaller, clean deep-indigo sans serif; keep it readable and secondary without a separate colored panel. The title and subtitle are crisp, flat 2D overlays independent of the press drawing, not printed onto its paper bed. Make the press, title, and subtitle read as one intentional composition, with the type remaining the first read. No monumental architecture, one-sided hero prop, perfume or flower styling, velvet or silk, amber haze, fake premium lighting, symmetry-for-its-own-sake, extra text, badges, decorative frames, orbit lines, glitter, sparkles, random flecks, or stacked effects.
