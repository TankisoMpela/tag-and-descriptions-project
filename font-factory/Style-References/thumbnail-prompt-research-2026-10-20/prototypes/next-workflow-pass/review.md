# Review — Next Workflow Pass

## Scope

The current prototype gallery contained 17 finished thumbnail concepts. The only two active raw white-background specimens without a thumbnail were Evercourt and Vantrelle. This folder now has three 3:2 images: two distinct Evercourt treatments the seller liked and one revised Vantrelle treatment.

## Workflow changes demonstrated

- The actual white-background specimen guides the font name's letterforms and natural capitalization.
- The font name takes visual priority, with one short, readable style descriptor beneath it.
- The image idea comes from the font, its name, or a meaningful type-making context rather than a default luxury aesthetic.
- The workflow compares positive and negative user examples as preference signals, not templates. It allows photography, illustration, or graphic design when the concept is specific and balanced.
- Strong local contrast and title focus take priority over decorative detail.
- Type stays a flat overlay, separate from depicted objects or surfaces.

## User preference signals captured

The seller liked both Evercourt versions, Quill Crest, Story Wisp, Running Saffron, Silken Flourish, and Ember Bracket. Across those varied styles, the common qualities are clear contrast, strong typography, and visual ideas that fit the font or name. These examples do not define a single palette or layout to reproduce.

The seller rejected repeated warm, hazy, staged imagery with a large face or object pushed to one side and open title space opposite it. Backgrounds themselves are not the problem: an illustration, photo, or print texture works when it contributes to the idea and the lettering stays the first read. Overly dominant silk texture, abstract arrangements without a clear idea, and sparse low-contrast fields are also risks.

These preferences are recorded in `../prompt-findings.md` and referenced by the Agent-27 creative workflow. No fixed side-composition or medium is imposed on future thumbnails.

## Concept and iteration record

- **Evercourt photographic option:** the first image-model output interpreted the tennis-court direction as a quiet photograph. The seller liked it and the printed illustration; both are now included as separate options.
- **Evercourt screen-printed option:** a limited-color illustration of a neighborhood tennis court. It is visibly printed and keeps the calligraphic title large over the dark tree line.
- **Vantrelle revision:** the seller identified the architectural photograph as predictable, fake-premium, and too weighted to one side. It was replaced by a hand-printed letterpress illustration, connecting the background to typography itself. The machine spans the lower frame while the serif title fills the open central area.

## Visual review

- All names and descriptors use natural title case and are spelled as specified.
- The font name is the largest read and remains fully inside each frame.
- Evercourt's photo and print options both keep the script clear and use a relevant tennis setting.
- Vantrelle uses a light paper field behind deep-indigo type, a vermilion press accent, and a type-making illustration without a large one-sided hero object.
- The Vantrelle title preserves the specimen's high-contrast display-serifs instead of using a generic bold serif.
- The three images are linked individually from `index.html`; there is no collage.
