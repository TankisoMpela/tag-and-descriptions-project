# Thumbnail Review — 17-Font Sweep

## What changed

The current gallery has one separate 3:2 image for each of 17 fonts. Eight additional fonts were designed from their own white-background specimens. The earlier nine thumbnails were refreshed so each 3:2 render now includes a descriptor line, matching the role of the square versions. Silken Flourish has a new flat poster concept, and Neon Parade uses title case to match its connected script's natural casing. Five renders replace repetitive premium still-life backdrops with lettering-led compositions, using modest paper/ink texture or a small relevant illustration where a plain field felt sterile. Three rejected iterations are archived; they are not style references for future prompts.

## Visual checks

All 17 files are 1536 × 1024 PNGs. Each rendered image was inspected at full size during generation and as a reduced view for title prominence, small-size hierarchy, clipping, descriptor presence, and separation of lettering from depicted surfaces. The five refreshed images use no product-photo background. The prototype folder contains the 17 individual images, not a collage.

The names and subtitles use title case except for Kuro Ripper, Ember Bracket, and Aureus Clare, whose specimens use naturally uppercase forms. Descriptor copy is intentionally short and descriptive. The new-font set tests distinctly different layouts and visual subjects rather than applying one design to eight fonts.

Image generation used the built-in image-generation tool with the corresponding white-background specimens. The tool does not expose its provider/model identity, so this work is not described as an Imagen test. The scope is 3:2 thumbnail prototypes; it does not replace square or storefront renders.

## Working rules retained

- Read the font specimen and choose a composition that fits its actual letterforms.
- Include one concise, readable descriptor beneath or near the title.
- Preserve natural title case; keep all-caps only for typefaces whose specimens are designed that way.
- Keep lettering flat, front-facing, and separate from photographed or rendered surfaces.
- Use one clear subject or graphic idea, strong contrast, and enough title scale to read at thumbnail size.
- Compare each new concept with recent thumbnails across medium, layout, focal motif, palette, texture/light, and title placement. Avoid repeating a generic premium still life or the rejected cropped-initial/orbit, blue/berry split-panel, and arch/portal treatments. A restrained flat design may use one purposeful paper/ink texture or faint illustration; vary the visual idea, not only the palette.
- Avoid glitter, sparkles, AI-like filler detail, decorative extras, and repeated layouts.
- Inspect both the full image and a reduced view before making a render current.
