TEXT LAYOUT AND WORD INTEGRITY — HARD REQUIREMENT
Because this square may be generated programmatically, inspect every visible
word and text block before finalizing the canvas. No word may be clipped by the
canvas edge, hidden by artwork, or broken by an accidental line wrap. If a
word is cut off or split across lines, fix the composition before output:
widen the text area, adjust tracking or line spacing, or reflow only at
complete-word boundaries. Treat reducing the type size or moving the block as a
last resort and use the smallest change that restores legibility -- the
placeholder's type geometry is the composition (see TEXT PLACEHOLDER LAYOUT).
Never accept a clipped, half-rendered, or unintentionally hyphenated word.
Every required word must be fully visible and legible at the final square size.

THEME VARIANT HANDOFF — REQUIRED
Use the pasted `Theme variant` for this exact image (the section under this
image's `##` heading in `theme.txt`) as the creative north star for this square
panel. It is the Agent 27-authored direction that tailors the font's shared
flat 2D world — art language, palette family, atmosphere root — to this board's
own lettering, copy, layout, and role. Translate it into the art or visual
language, palette/color relationships, atmosphere, and energy or communication
mode of the supporting copy. It is direction for the font's visual world, not text to print on the
image.

Do not repeat the variant verbatim, reuse one hardcoded phrase across
every panel, or turn it into a rigid square template. Choose copy and hierarchy
for this particular source image and make this square materially differentiated
from the other supporting images and from the 3:2 version. A short sentence,
one full-screen word, a label, a headline, a compact editorial line, or another
purposeful format may be right depending on the font and source. If the variant
is missing or blank, fall back to the global `Supporting-image theme sentence`;
if both are missing, stop and ask the user to paste one rather than
inventing a replacement theme.

TEXT PLACEHOLDER LAYOUT — DO NOT MOVE OR RESIZE THE TYPE
The supplied text placeholder for this ratio is the art-directed type
composition. Its type size, position, alignment, line breaks, and visual
prominence are the brief, not a draft to improve. Build the artwork around the
type; never recompose the type to suit the artwork.

- Do not move, resize, re-crop, re-stack, re-align, re-order, or shrink the
  typography.
- The square placeholder has its own layout. Honouring it is not the same as
  cropping the 3:2 image -- this panel is square, its type geometry is whatever
  the square placeholder shows, and that geometry is fixed.
- Do not shrink the type to make room for artwork. Take space from the artwork,
  never from the type.
- Keep the type exactly as visually dominant as it is in the placeholder. The
  type is the product; the artwork is the context around it.
- The type's colour, material, and treatment may be adapted. Its geometry may
  not.
- The one exception: if the type would otherwise be clipped, hidden, or
  illegible, change the minimum geometry needed, keeping the same relative
  position, alignment, and prominence. Never reduce its size for composition.

PRIMARY TASK
Reimagine the old supporting image as one complete, finished 1:1 supporting

broad purpose, then make your own creative choices. Preserve useful product
meaning where appropriate. Make a materially new concept rather than a crop,
recolor, reskin, or padded version of the old image or the 3:2 image.

WHITE-BOARD TRANSFORMATION OVERRIDE — STRICT
When the supplied old image is a white-background text placeholder, do not
recreate that blank white board in the square output. Treat it as raw material
for the font lettering, copy, hierarchy, and broad purpose only. A white field
with the same text simply placed on it — or a realistic object behind the same
layout — fails.

Build a complete square marketplace design with a deliberate visual treatment:
use a considered color/background, abstract flat graphic art, editorial grid or
rules, paper/ink/print texture, restrained illustration, or a controlled 2D
graphic texture that suits this font. Keep the typography exactly where the
square placeholder puts it, at the size it gives it -- do not resize, move,
stack, crop, align, or reorder it. Design the field, artwork, and texture around
that fixed type geometry. Keep the font lettering large and legible at small
gallery size.
White may be one designed element, but it cannot be the untouched default
canvas. The result should look like a finished type specimen, poster, label,
flat package-art panel, editorial card, or other credible font application — not a
text overlay waiting for design.

SQUARE COMPOSITION
- Design the composition as a square from the beginning; do not crop or derive
  it from the 3:2 image.
- Keep all important lettering and copy inside generous safe margins and fully
  visible at small preview size.
- Use one clear presentation of the font lettering, with a coherent visual
  relationship between the type, background, and supporting copy.
- The font lettering must remain flat and front-facing: no extrusion, inflation,
  sculpting, freestanding 3D lettering, or lettering wrapped around cylinders,
  boxes, walls, signs, plinths, or any object with depth.
- Only flat editorial design, poster/label artwork, 2D collage, illustration,
  and controlled graphic paper/ink textures are allowed. Photography, physical
  packaging or paper applications, and abstract glossy 3D concept art are
  forbidden.
- Keep the visual world deliberately simple. Prefer a solid color or flat graphic
  field, quiet editorial grid, 2D illustration, poster/label artwork, or one
  restrained graphic motif. Simplicity is intentional art direction, not an
  unfinished result.
- **No decorative shadow or dappled light.** Do not add cast shadows, leaf or
  foliage shadows, blind or window-frame shadows, wall shadows, or a soft shadow
  gradient laid over the composition. This is the most overused way to make a
  panel read as "premium", it appears by default, and it is not a concept. Include
  a real light-and-shadow effect only when the composition's own idea depends on
  it, never as atmosphere or a finishing layer. Do not substitute mood lighting,
  glow, grain, or paper texture for the same effect.
- Do not use photography, rooms, busy landscapes, cinematic set dressing, piles
  of props, physical materials, or multiple interacting objects.
- Use no more than two supporting graphic elements besides the lettering and
  required copy. Do not add objects merely to make the image feel finished.
  Flat color, paper/ink texture used as 2D art, simple geometry, restrained
  illustration, and abstract flat art are the required alternatives to 3D
  environments.
- Preserve the source's truthful product purpose and as much legible truthful
  subtext as possible. Remove old branding, URLs, handles, unsupported claims,
  and unreadable text. Do not add unprovided attribution or studio copy.
- Change at least four meaningful decisions from the old reference, including
  graphic context, flat visual language, background composition, crop of the
  artwork, palette, copy treatment, or secondary motifs. The typography's
  placement and size are not among the decisions you may change.
- Make the pasted theme variant evident through the square's art/visual
  language, palette, atmosphere, and copy energy, while keeping this panel
  distinct from the other supporting images.
- Reimagining does not require visual complexity: a materially new color field,
  close-up graphic crop, paper/ink study rendered as 2D art, or minimal graphic
  composition is valid and preferred
  when it communicates the font clearly.

UNSEEN GLYPH AND TYPOGRAPHY BOUNDARY — SQUARE OVERRIDE
Use the product font only for glyphs and lettering visibly supplied in the
attached font seed/specimen or white reference board. Do not infer unseen
letterforms, complete a word with guessed glyphs, extrapolate the style to
unsupported letters or symbols, or redraw product lettering from memory. If
new supporting copy needs characters that were not supplied, render it in
clearly generic auxiliary typography that is visibly different and secondary;
never make it look like evidence of the product font.

OUTPUT
- Generate exactly one complete image in one generation/editing operation.
- Target 1:1, preferably 1500x1500, 1536x1536, or 1600x1600.
- Do not create a 3:2 image, Pinterest image, collage, or long-scroll panel.
- Do not use post-processing or an overlay to repair spelling, glyph fidelity,
  or missing copy. Regenerate the complete image if the result fails.

FINAL SELF-CHECK
Reject and regenerate if the product lettering is generic, misspelled,
distorted, guessed from unseen glyphs, clipped, incorrectly colored for the
composition, or rendered as 3D; if any photograph, live-action environment,
physical object, perspective surface, product mockup, or depth staging appears;
if the
result is merely a crop/reskin; if it merely recreates a white text board or
leaves the typography on an empty white canvas without a designed
background/graphic treatment; if the typography was moved, resized, or
re-cropped from the placeholder, or made less dominant than it was; if truthful relevant copy was unnecessarily dropped; if old
branding or invented claims appear; or if the output is not 1:1.
Also reject it if the composition does not express the pasted theme variant
through its art, palette, atmosphere, and copy energy, makes every supporting
panel feel the same, or prints the theme variant as a slogan.
